## What the live pages showed

- **Lists load only in front.** ChatGPT's sidebar: 31 in a background tab, 47 in front. The
  popup's Gather works on the tab in front; the Gather tab's own loading, and gathering in its
  window, must keep the page shown too.
- **Long chats are virtualized** (ChatGPT `[data-virtualized-turn]`, DeepSeek
  `.ds-virtual-list`): the page holds the turns near the screen only. So counting elements
  says nothing - it stays about the same while older turns come and newer ones go - and reading
  once at the end loses all but the last screen.

## So, a long chat

1. Read what is on the page, keep each turn by a key of its own (ChatGPT's `data-turn-key`,
   DeepSeek's `data-virtual-list-item-key`, else the text), in page order.
2. Scroll the transcript's box up, as far as it goes now.
3. Pause, for the webapp to load.
4. Is the box able to go further up (its `scrollTop` above 0 again, or its `scrollHeight` grown)?
   Then again from 1. If not, a second pause and one more try before taking it as the start.
5. The turns kept, put in order (older ones were met later), are the chat.

The same for a list: keep every link seen (done in RR8), and the end is where the box cannot
go further down after a pause.

## For the gate

A `scroll` tool (a box by selector, to top or bottom, giving `scrollTop`/`scrollHeight` before
and after) would let the agent watch a webapp load, instead of guessing from counts.

## Correction, later on 2026-11-28

"Only in front" was wrong. With the tab brought to the front first, one load gave 31 again;
a second load right after it gave 47. What happened the first time: the background run
stopped at 31, the run "in front" started from there and reached 47. Each run **stops too
early**, and the next goes on where it stopped. Why it stops is to be watched with the gate's
`scroll` tool: the sidebar's box, its position and height over time, after each pause.

## Second correction: the correction was wrong

Tried cleanly with the gate's `scroll` tool and fresh tabs:
- the sidebar scrolled by hand to its bottom, 2 s each: 16, 21, 26, 31, 36, 41, 47 - five a step;
- `list` with `load` on a fresh tab in front: 47 in one run, 15-17 s;
- on a fresh tab in the background: 21, nothing loaded;
- in the background, the tab brought to the front by the background first: 47 in one run.

So the first note was right: ChatGPT loads more of its list only while its page is shown. The
"31 with the tab in front" of the first correction was a run that started before `task run`'s
reload had brought the change in. Lesson: after a change, check the reload happened (the
manifest's time, the host restarted) before trying it.


## Long chats, tried live (later on 2026-11-28)

On one long ChatGPT chat, with the gate's `progress`, `scroll` and `read` (`whole: true`). Each
try taught one thing; the reader (kit `readWhole`) now does all of them:

1. **Jump to the top** (`scrollTop = 0`): ChatGPT loads older messages, but in a virtualized
   page a jump skips what lies between; a chat loaded already comes out with holes. So: from
   the newest end, **one screen up at a time**, each window read.
2. **The box**: the nearest box that scrolls around the first message was a long user message
   scrolling inside itself; 423 rounds moved nothing. So: the box around the transcript (or
   around all messages).
3. **At the top, nothing asked**: a box already at its top does not ask the webapp for more;
   it stopped at 10. So: a step down and back up by as much (not to 0: what loaded meanwhile
   moved the view), then the pause.
4. **Patience**: ChatGPT brings the older ones 2-5 s after the top, some later. So: 3 s at
   the top, three quiet pauses before the start is taken.
5. **The join**: a window was joined only at the oldest message kept, which a virtualized page
   may no longer hold: 600 rounds, 12 kept. So: at the first message the window shares with
   what is kept; and a message known by the page's **id** (Sett's scrapnote: ChatGPT's
   `data-chatgpt-search-unit-key`), text only as the fallback.
6. **Pushed back**: ChatGPT measures the messages above as they come into view and pushes the
   view down, again and again; the top was never reached. So: after 40 steps without a new
   message, straight to the top (all below is read); "can it go further" told by the chat
   grown taller, not by the view kept off the top; and 60 s without a new message ends it.

After the sixth: 110 messages and rising, about 10 every 20 s - ChatGPT's own pace.

The sixth's run went on: 500 messages after 17 min, still rising when the helper's 15 min ran out
(the reader went on in the page until the next reload). Not yet seen to its end; speed is next.

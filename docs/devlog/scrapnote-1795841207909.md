> did you know that very long conversation history is never rendered entirely? only visible stuff like i…

Yes, the live pages showed exactly that. ChatGPT and DeepSeek only render what's near the screen, like culling in a 3D game: the rest of the story isn't in the page at all until you scroll to it. That's why one plain read of that long ChatGPT chat got only the last 8 messages.

The whole read now in progress does part of what you describe:
- It starts from the newest end and steps up one screen at a time, reading each window.
- It joins each window to what it already has where they overlap.
- At the top, it pauses and checks whether the box can go further into the past (your method).

Where your hint goes further than the current code is the **ids**. Right now a message is recognised by its role and text. Two identical messages ("ok", "yes") could be merged into one, or mismatch the join between windows. The pages do give real ids:

- ChatGPT: `data-turn-key="<uuid>"` on each turn, plus `data-chatgpt-search-unit-key` and `data-chatgpt-search-message-ids` on each message.
- DeepSeek: `data-virtual-list-item-key` on each item.

Keying by those ids, with text only as a fallback for webapps that have none, is the sounder way to decide "seen this one already, or is it new to the story". It's a good next refinement for the join, once the read now running shows that the windowing itself works on the live chat.
